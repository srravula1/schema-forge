## Section 1 — What your firm does

**Describe your firm's primary service offering in one to two sentences, as you would to a warm referral (not a cold prospect).**

> _Answer:_ We help mid-market B2B companies — primarily SaaS and professional services firms — design and ship production-grade AI systems: LLM-powered workflows, RAG pipelines, and evaluation/governance infrastructure that their existing engineering team can own and maintain after we leave. We do the hard part up front so their team is not stuck maintaining a black box.

**List the specific engagement types you sell (e.g., "fractional CTO," "AI readiness assessment," "LLM fine-tuning retainer," "implementation sprint"). Be as granular as you can — use the names your sales team uses internally, not marketing copy.**

> _Answer:_
> - **AI Readiness Audit** — 2-week assessment, $15k–25k, deliverable is a gap analysis and 90-day implementation roadmap. Our "foot in the door" product.
> - **RAG Implementation Sprint** — 6–10 week engagement, $60k–120k, we build and deploy a production RAG system on their data. Most common first paid project.
> - **LLM Evaluation & Governance Setup** — 4–6 weeks, $45k–80k, we instrument their existing LLM usage with evals, red-teaming, and a model-swap decision framework. Usually follows a RAG sprint.
> - **AI Architecture Retainer** — monthly, $15k–25k/mo, 3-month minimum. Ongoing advisory + hands-on for teams that shipped something and now need to scale it. Rare but high-value.
> - **Fractional AI Lead** — weekly embedded hours, $8k–15k/mo. Fills a gap when a client is between a VP Eng departure and a permanent AI hire.

**What problem does a client typically have right before they hire you? Describe the situation, not the category.**

> _Answer:_ The most common situation: their board or CEO has said "we need to do something with AI this quarter" and they have an engineering team but no one with LLM production experience. They may have a POC a junior engineer built that works in a notebook but has never seen real traffic. They need someone who has done this before to take it from prototype to production without 6 months of trial and error. The second most common: they built something with a vendor and it's underperforming — hallucinations, wrong answers, bad retrieval — and they don't know if the problem is the model, the data, or the architecture.

## Section 2 — Deal economics

**What is your typical deal size range (in USD)?**

> _Minimum deal:_ $15,000 (AI Readiness Audit)
> _Typical deal:_ $75,000 (RAG sprint or eval/governance setup)
> _Largest deal to date:_ $280,000 (multi-phase: audit -> sprint -> retainer for 8 months)

**What is your typical sales cycle length — from first contact to signed contract?**

> _Answer:_ 3–8 weeks for the Readiness Audit. 6–12 weeks for a sprint. The audit almost always shortens the sprint cycle because trust is established. Cold-contact-to-sprint-signed with no prior relationship is 10–14 weeks minimum, sometimes longer if procurement is involved.

**Is payment typically a one-time project fee, a monthly retainer, or milestone-based?**

> _Answer:_ Fixed-fee, paid in two installments: 50% on signing, 50% on delivery of the defined milestone. Retainers are monthly, paid in advance. We don't do time-and-materials — every engagement has a defined scope and a fixed price.

## Section 4 — Competitive landscape

**Name 2–4 firms or alternatives a prospect might consider instead of you. These can be direct competitors, DIY alternatives, or "doing nothing."**

> 1. Hire a full-time AI engineer or Head of AI instead (the most common "alternative" — takes 4–6 months to hire, and the new hire often lands and is equally lost without a system in place)
> 2. Large consulting firms (McKinsey QuantumBlack, BCG X, Accenture AI Studio) — lose deals here when the client needs enterprise credibility or is doing a 7-figure transformation initiative, not a sprint
> 3. Other boutique AI consultancies (usually 2–5 person shops, often ex-FAANG) — lose to these on price when budget is tight or when the prospect has a warm referral to someone specific
> 4. "Doing it ourselves with ChatGPT / Cursor" — the most common reason a deal stalls and comes back 6 months later after they've made a mess

**What is the most common reason a prospect chooses you over those alternatives?**

> _Answer:_ Speed and accountability. We have shipped production RAG systems, not just POCs. We can point to specific clients who went from prototype to production in 8 weeks. The alternatives are either slower (hiring), more expensive (Big 3 consulting), or riskier (DIY). Our second differentiator: we write documentation and transfer knowledge so the client's team can own it — we don't build a dependency on us. Clients who have been burned by a black-box vendor care a lot about this.

**What is the most common reason a prospect chooses an alternative over you?**

> _Answer:_ Price, when the client is undercapitalized. If they're pre-Series A and trying to do this on a shoestring, they're not our buyer. The second reason: when they already have a warm referral to a specific person at a competing boutique — loyalty to a specific engineer they know and trust is hard to overcome, and we usually don't try.

## Section 6 — Anything else

**Is there anything about your business, your clients, or your sales process that you think an outsider would consistently misunderstand or underestimate?**

> _Answer:_ Two things. First: people assume the problem is budget. It's not, usually. The problem is project readiness. A company can have $100k approved and still not be ready for us — because they have no data prepared, no internal owner identified, and no clear success criterion. We've learned to disqualify on "project readiness" before we even ask about budget. If they can't tell us what "good" looks like at the end of the engagement, we don't take the project.
>
> Second: LinkedIn followers and post engagement are not the same as buyer intent. We get a lot of enthusiasm from people who love the content — founders of 3-person startups, ML engineers at big companies exploring on their own, students. They engage, they ask questions, they DM us. Almost none of them are buyers. The people who become buyers usually lurk for weeks, sometimes months, and then send a very specific, business-specific question about a problem they're currently facing. That specificity is the real signal.

**Is there anything you want us to know before the first interview that is not covered above?**

> _Answer:_ Our pricing is a real filter. $50k+ projects self-select for companies with real budgets. We've tried discounting for early-stage companies and it ends badly — they have less internal process, less data infrastructure, and less tolerance for ambiguity, which means the project costs us more in time than the discount covered. Series A+ or bootstrapped with positive cash flow is our floor for the $50k+ sprint engagements.
