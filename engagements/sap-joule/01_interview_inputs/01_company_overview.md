## Section 1 — What your firm does

**Describe your firm's primary service offering in one to two sentences, as you would to a warm referral (not a cold prospect).**

> We run $150k–$500k Joule and AI-implementation pilots for companies already running SAP — helping them activate SAP's native AI layer (Joule, BTP AI Core, GenAI Hub) on their existing ERP footprint rather than bolt-on third-party tools. The typical outcome is a working Joule use case in production within 12–16 weeks, with a handoff plan to the client's internal SAP BASIS and IT teams.

**List the specific engagement types you sell (e.g., "fractional CTO," "AI readiness assessment," "LLM fine-tuning retainer," "implementation sprint"). Be as granular as you can — use the names your sales team uses internally, not marketing copy.**

> - **SAP Joule Readiness Assessment** ($40k–$60k, 4 weeks): baseline SAP landscape audit (ECC vs. S/4, module footprint, clean-core score, BTP entitlements), produce a prioritized Joule use-case backlog with ROI estimates.
> - **Joule Pilot Sprint** ($150k–$250k, 12 weeks): one to two production-ready Joule use cases deployed (e.g., intelligent goods receipt, AI-assisted FI period-close), change management playbook, go-forward roadmap.
> - **S/4HANA Migration + AI Uplift** ($250k–$500k, 16–24 weeks): parallel-track S/4HANA greenfield/brownfield migration with Joule and BTP AI Core configured from day one. Typically pulled in by a Big4 transformation program as a specialist workstream.
> - **AI-in-ERP Governance Advisory** ($80k, 6 weeks): AI risk assessment for Joule outputs (hallucination controls, audit log configuration, data residency), delivered standalone or as a precursor to the pilot sprint.

**What problem does a client typically have right before they hire you? Describe the situation, not the category.**

> The CFO or CIO has seen SAP's Joule demo at SAPPHIRE or from their SAP Account Executive and asked "why aren't we doing this?" The IT team has no in-house Joule or BTP AI Core expertise. The company is either on S/4HANA and doesn't know where to start, or still on ECC with a funded S/4 migration in the next 1–3 years and wants to build AI into the migration program. The pressure is real: SAP is sunsetting ECC support in 2027, so there's a board-level forcing function.

**What is your typical sales cycle length — from first contact to signed contract?**

> 60–120 days. SAP-field co-sell shortens this to 45–75 days when the SAP AE is active. Big4 pull-through bypasses the sales cycle — we're brought in as a named subcontractor on a signed prime contract.

**Is payment typically a one-time project fee, a monthly retainer, or milestone-based?**

## Section 3 — Who pays you

**What is the job title of the person who typically signs the contract?**

> CIO, CFO, or CTO. For Big4-pull-through engagements, the prime contractor (e.g., Deloitte or Accenture) is the direct signatory; the end-customer approves the subcontractor clause.

**What is the job title of the person who typically champions the deal internally (often different from the signer)?**

> SAP Program Director, VP of IT Transformation, or Director of Enterprise Architecture. In companies without a named SAP Program Director, the Head of Finance (if the primary use case is FI/CO) or Head of Supply Chain (if MM/SD/PP) often champions.

**What is the job title of the person your team speaks with most during the sales process?**

> SAP Center of Excellence Lead or SAP BASIS/Platform Manager — they control the BTP entitlements, understand the module footprint, and are the technical gatekeeper. On the budget side, the CIO or CFO's EA gates the meeting calendar.

## Section 4 — Competitive landscape

**Name 2–4 firms or alternatives a prospect might consider instead of you. These can be direct competitors, DIY alternatives, or "doing nothing."**

> 1. Accenture SAP Practice / Deloitte S/4HANA COE (large SI — typical fallback for companies that want name-brand risk cover)
> 2. NTT Data SAP Joule implementation team (tier-2 SI with a Joule-specific practice)
> 3. SAP's own consulting arm (SAP Consulting, formerly SAP MaxAttention) — often proposed by the SAP AE as a bundled option
> 4. "Build it internally with SAP Learning Hub" — technically possible, practically rare; used as a budget-pressure negotiation tactic

**What is the most common reason a prospect chooses you over those alternatives?**

> Speed and specificity. A Big4 SAP practice quotes 18–24 months for a transformation including AI uplift; we deliver a working Joule use case in 12 weeks. Prospects who need to show the board an AI win within the fiscal year consistently choose us. The second reason is module-depth: we have deeper FI/CO and SD expertise than most boutique Joule shops.

**What is the most common reason a prospect chooses an alternative over you?**

> Risk aversion at the CFO level: "We need a firm with 50,000 SAP consultants, not 40." The large-SI incumbent relationship also kills deals — if Deloitte is already running the SAP program, adding a subcontractor requires a political fight the internal champion doesn't want.

## Section 6 — Anything else

**Is there anything about your business, your clients, or your sales process that you think an outsider would consistently misunderstand or underestimate?**

> The biggest misunderstanding: SAP AE excitement does NOT mean there is a deal. An SAP account executive gets quota credit for co-sells and has every incentive to refer us into an account, even if the end-customer has zero Joule budget or their S/4 migration is 3 years away. We have burned multiple quarters chasing "hot co-sells" that were really just the AE managing their pipeline optics. We now require end-customer budget evidence before any co-sell goes active in our CRM.
>
> Second: ECC presence ≠ near-term buying signal. Half the Fortune 1000 still runs ECC. The qualifier is not "do they run SAP?" — it's "do they have a funded S/4 migration or a board mandate to activate Joule within 18 months?" Without that, they are a multi-year nurture.
