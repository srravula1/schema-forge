## Section 1 — Account-level ICP

**Describe your best customer in 3–5 sentences.**

> Best customer: a manufacturing, chemicals, retail, or life sciences company running SAP S/4HANA (on-prem or RISE) with 1,000–15,000 employees, $500M–$5B revenue, and a named S/4HANA program or active migration in the next 18 months. They have a SAP Center of Excellence with at least 3 FTEs and a BTP entitlement already in place (or in procurement). The CIO or CFO has received a Joule demo from their SAP AE and has asked the IT team to evaluate. They have at least one complex, high-volume SAP process (FI/CO period-close, MM goods receipt/invoice matching, SD order processing, or PP/QM quality workflows) where current manual steps create measurable cost or cycle-time pressure.

**What is the minimum firmographic profile you would accept (the floor before DQ)?**

> - Running SAP (ECC or S/4HANA) — non-SAP accounts are a hard DQ.
> - Employee count >= 500 (SAP implementations below this rarely justify AI complexity).
> - Revenue >= $100M (proxy for $150k+ pilot budget capacity).
> - At least one named Joule or S/4HANA AI use case articulated by the prospect.
> - Pilot budget floor: $150k confirmed or in-process.

**What industries do you have the most evidence in?**

> Manufacturing (discrete and process): 40% of pipeline; FI/CO + PP/QM footprint.
> Chemicals: 20%; FI/CO + MM + SD.
> Retail: 15%; SD + MM + EWM; RISE with SAP preferred.
> Life Sciences: 15%; FI/CO + QM; GROW with SAP or S/4 Cloud.
> Energy and Utilities: 10%; early pipeline; FI/CO + PM.

**What tech-stack signals most reliably predict a viable prospect?**

> 1. SAP S/4HANA (any variant) — high signal; prerequisite for most Joule use cases.
> 2. RISE with SAP subscription — very high signal; includes BTP entitlements.
> 3. SAP BTP with AI Core entitlement — very high signal; infrastructure is paid for.
> 4. SAP ECC 6.0 with documented S/4 roadmap or funded migration — medium-high signal.
> 5. SAP ECC 6.0 with no S/4 roadmap — low signal; nurture; re-engage 12-18 months before forced sunset.
> 6. Non-SAP ERP (Oracle, Workday, Dynamics) — hard DQ for Joule work.

## Section 2 — Contact-level ICP

**Describe your buyer persona(s) by title, seniority, and department.**

End-customer personas:
> - CIO (c_suite, engineering/other) — primary signer. Needs board-level ROI narrative.
> - CFO (c_suite, finance) — co-signs when use case is FI/CO-focused.
> - VP of IT / VP of Enterprise Architecture (vp, engineering) — operational champion, owns SAP roadmap.
> - SAP Program Director / Head of SAP CoE (director, engineering/operations) — day-to-day champion.
> - Head of Finance / Controller (director, finance) — champion for FI/CO use cases.

SAP-field personas (co-sell channel, NOT end-customer buyers; org-affiliation: sap_field):
> - SAP Regional VP of Sales (seniority: vp, dept: sales) — refers firm into accounts; motivated by co-sell quota.
> - SAP VP of Sales / Area VP (seniority: vp, dept: sales) — oversight on strategic accounts.
> - SAP Account Executive (seniority: ic/manager, dept: sales) — direct referral, manages co-sell day-to-day.

SI-partner personas (pull-through channel, NOT end-customer buyers; org-affiliation: si_partner):
> - Managing Director, SAP Practice (seniority: c_suite, dept: other) — Big4 MD, decides subcontracting.
> - Partner, SAP/Digital Transformation (seniority: vp, dept: other) — day-to-day on the Big4 engagement.

**What seniority levels consistently generate deals?**

> c_suite and vp level on end-customer side (CIO, CFO, VP IT). Director-level (SAP Program Director) is day-to-day champion but rarely signs alone. IC-level end-customer contacts (BASIS admin, ABAP developer) are technical validators only.

**What departments consistently generate deals?**

> IT/Engineering (SAP Platform, Enterprise Architecture, CoE) and Finance (FI/CO use case). Supply Chain/Operations when use case is MM/SD/PP. C-suite for deal closure.

## Section 3 — Deal patterns

**Most recent closed-won deals:**

> P1 — Chemicals manufacturer, $285k, 14 weeks. Source: SAP RVP co-sell. S/4HANA on-prem, ~2,400 SAP users, FI/CO + MM. Use case: AI-assisted month-end close variance analysis. CIO signed, SAP Program Director championed.
> P2 — Retail chain, $410k, 18 weeks. Source: Deloitte pull-through. RISE with SAP, ~900 SAP users, SD + MM. Use case: Joule-assisted order exception management. CFO + VP Supply Chain co-signed.
> P3 — Life sciences, $175k, 10 weeks. Source: CIO referral from P1. S/4 Cloud public (GROW), ~280 SAP users, FI/CO only. Use case: Joule financial planning narrative. CIO signed directly.

**Most recent lost or regretted deals:**

> L1 — Large energy company, DQ'd after Readiness Assessment. ECC 6.0, no S/4 roadmap, ~6,500 SAP users. SAP AE was over-enthusiastic; account was 2+ years away from being Joule-ready. Cost 60 days.
> L2 — Mid-size manufacturer, lost to Accenture SAP practice. S/4HANA on-prem, ~1,800 SAP users, FI/CO + PP. CFO chose Accenture on delivery risk perception. No win against Big4 incumbent with risk-averse CFO.
> L3 — SAP AE-referred retail company. Spent 45 days in discovery. End-customer CIO "supportive in principle" but no allocated budget. Classic channel mirage: AE excitement did not equal end-customer deal.

**Most reliable early disqualification signal?**

> "We're still evaluating whether SAP is the right platform for AI" = 12-18 months away. No named use case after two discovery sessions = tire-kicker. SAP AE excited but no end-customer budget confirmation = channel mirage, DQ from active pipeline.

## Section 4 — Anti-ICP / negative signals

**Clearest signals that an account is NOT a fit:**

> 1. Running non-SAP ERP — hard DQ.
> 2. ECC 6.0 with no funded S/4 migration and no board mandate for ECC sunset — nurture only.
> 3. Below $150k pilot budget stated explicitly — DQ; below economics floor.
> 4. No named AI use case after two discovery sessions — tire-kicker; move to nurture.
> 5. SAP field contact as only touch, no end-customer contact engaged — channel mirage risk; hold.
> 6. Company < 500 employees — rarely has SAP infrastructure that justifies pilot complexity.

## Section 5 — The outsider question

> Outsiders would be surprised how many pipeline accounts are SAP AE co-sell nominations with no verified end-customer budget. The pipeline looks active from outside (lots of co-sell meetings, SAP-field engagement) but conversion from "SAP AE referred" to "end-customer signed" is only ~22%. The bottleneck: is the end-customer's IT or Finance leadership actually engaged with confirmed budget? Without that, we are doing SAP AE relationship management, not sales.
